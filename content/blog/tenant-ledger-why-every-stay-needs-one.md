Many owners keep one running balance for each tenant. It looks simple, but it breaks the moment a tenant moves out, comes back, or lives in two rooms.

## The problem with one balance per person

- A tenant leaves with an old due, comes back after six months, and the old due is mixed with the new rent.
- A tenant moves from room 101 to room 204, and nobody is sure which room the advance belongs to.
- A person is in two beds at once, for example with a family member, and the money cannot be separated.

## A ledger for each stay

A better rule is: **money belongs to a stay, never to the person.** Each stay has its own invoices, payments, advance and balance.

- **Balance = invoices not yet paid − advance − anything written off.** A positive number means the tenant owes you. A negative number means you hold their advance.
- **Moving out with a due:** you decide to collect it, write it off or keep it pending. A pending due stays on that old stay only.
- **Coming back later:** you open a new stay that starts at zero. The old stay keeps its own history.
- **Changing room or bed:** close the old stay and open a new one.
- **A mistake in a phone number:** change it once on the person, and it updates everywhere.

## Why this helps your accounts

Your dues list is built from stays, so an old pending due keeps showing until it is collected or written off. Nothing disappears just because the tenant left.
