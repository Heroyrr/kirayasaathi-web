A clear rent agreement protects both you and your tenant. This is a general checklist, not legal advice. Rules differ by state, so please check yours or ask a lawyer.

## What to include

1. **Names and details.** Full names, addresses and phone numbers of the owner and the tenant, and the property address.
2. **Rent and due date.** The monthly rent, the date it is due, and how it should be paid.
3. **Security deposit.** The amount, and when and how it will be returned.
4. **Period.** The start date and the length of the agreement.
5. **Increase in rent.** If and when the rent will go up, and by how much.
6. **Notice period.** How much notice each side must give before leaving or ending the agreement.
7. **Lock-in period** (if any). Say what happens if the tenant leaves early.
8. **Electricity, water and maintenance.** Who pays, and how the bills are shared.
9. **House rules.** Visitors, noise, smoking, pets, timings in a PG, and use of common areas.
10. **Repairs.** Who pays for what: small repairs, big repairs, damage by the tenant.
11. **Inspection.** When the owner may enter the property, with notice.
12. **Ending the agreement.** Reasons it can end, and what the tenant must do when moving out.
13. **Identity proof.** Attach copies of the tenant’s ID proof and keep them safely.

## Stamping and registration

Stamp duty and registration rules are set by each state. In many places, an agreement for longer than 11 months has to be registered, and shorter ones are often made on stamp paper. Check the rule in your state.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the agreement but never giving the tenant a copy.
- Leaving the deposit return conditions vague.
- Not writing the notice period.
- Accepting cash for rent without writing it down.
- Keeping the agreement only on paper that can get lost. Take a photo or scan and store it safely.
