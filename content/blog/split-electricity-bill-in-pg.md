Electricity is one of the biggest sources of arguments in PGs and hostels. The easiest way to avoid them is to use a clear method and to show the working.

## The simple formula

If each room has its own meter, use this:

**Electricity per person = (units used × rate per unit) ÷ number of people sharing the room**

## Example

- Last month’s reading: 1,200
- This month’s reading: 1,260
- Units used: 60
- Rate: ₹8 per unit
- People sharing the room: 3

The room’s bill is 60 × 8 = ₹480. Each person pays ₹480 ÷ 3 = ₹160.

## Tips for fairness

1. **Take a photo of the meter every month.** Take it on the same day each month and keep the photos. If anyone disputes the reading, you can show it.
2. **Never edit an old reading.** Add a new reading each time. A history of readings is proof that nothing was changed.
3. **Use one rate and tell everyone.** Use your actual per-unit cost from the electricity bill, or a fixed rate that you wrote in the agreement.
4. **Show the working on the invoice.** Write the start reading, the end reading, the rate and the number of people sharing.
5. **Handle a replaced meter carefully.** If a meter is replaced or reset, note it. The new reading may be lower than the old one, and that is fine if you mark it as a reset.
6. **Decide what happens when a tenant joins or leaves in the middle of the month.** Many owners charge by the days stayed. Whatever you choose, write it down.

## What if there is no meter in each room?

Then split the whole building’s bill by the number of people, or by a fixed monthly amount per bed that you include in the rent. A fixed amount is simpler, but check it against your real bills a few times a year.
