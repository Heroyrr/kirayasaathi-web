The security deposit is money you hold to cover unpaid rent, bills or damage. It causes many disputes because the rules are often unclear. Here is how to keep it simple and fair.

## How much to ask for

There is no single rule that applies everywhere. Practice differs by city and by type of property. Some places ask for one or two months of rent, and some ask for more. The Model Tenancy Act, 2021 suggests a limit of two months’ rent for homes, but it applies only in states that have adopted it. Check the rule in your state and decide an amount you can explain clearly.

## Put it in writing

Write these points in the agreement:

- The deposit amount.
- That it is a deposit and not rent. It cannot be treated as the last month’s rent unless you both agree.
- What it may be used for: unpaid rent, unpaid bills, damage beyond normal wear.
- When it will be returned, for example within 15 or 30 days after the tenant leaves.

## Record it properly

Write down the amount, the date, how it was paid and a reference. Give the tenant a receipt. Keep the deposit separate from the monthly rent in your accounts.

## When the tenant moves out

1. Check the room together and note the condition with photos.
2. Prepare a final invoice with any unpaid rent, electricity and other dues.
3. Subtract only what the agreement allows.
4. Return the balance in the way and in the time you promised, and write down the date and reference.

## Common mistakes

- Taking a deposit without a receipt.
- Not saying when it will be returned.
- Deducting for old wear and tear that is normal.
- Mixing the deposit with the rent in your records, which makes the final settlement confusing.
