Late rent hurts every PG owner. You still have to pay electricity, staff and loans, but the money arrives days or weeks late. The good news is that most late payments can be reduced with a few steady habits.

## 1. Fix one due date for everyone

Choose a clear due date, such as the 5th of every month, and write it in the agreement. When every tenant has a different date, you will forget some of them. If tenants join in the middle of the month, you can still charge from their start date, but keep the next due date the same for everyone.

## 2. Send a reminder before the due date, not after

A friendly message two or three days before the due date works better than an angry message after it. Most tenants are not refusing to pay. They simply forget. A short message with the amount and how to pay solves most cases.

## 3. Make paying easy

Accept UPI so the tenant can pay in a minute, and give the same UPI ID or QR code every month. If tenants have to ask you how to pay each time, they put it off.

## 4. Write down every payment with a reference

Record the amount, the date, the mode (cash, UPI or bank) and the reference number. When a tenant says “I already paid”, you can answer calmly with the record. Write down cash payments the same day.

## 5. Keep rent, deposit and advance separate

Never mix the security deposit with the monthly rent in your notes. An advance paid today should be adjusted against a future invoice, and a deposit should stay a deposit until the tenant leaves.

## 6. Send a proper invoice every month

An invoice that shows the rent, the electricity share and any extra charges leaves no room for doubt. If there is an old due or an advance, show it on the same invoice so the tenant sees one final amount to pay.

## 7. Follow up the next day

If the rent is not paid, message again the next day and note the date. Do not wait a week. Early, polite follow-ups keep small delays from becoming large dues.
