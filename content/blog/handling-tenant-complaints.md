Complaints are normal in any property: a leaking tap, a broken fan, no water, a noisy neighbour. What matters is how quickly you notice them and how clearly you follow up.

## A routine that works

1. **Write down every complaint.** Do not trust your memory. Note who complained, the room, the date and what the problem is.
2. **Ask for a photo.** A photo shows the problem and saves a visit. It also helps if the same problem returns later.
3. **Give it a status.** Use simple statuses like pending, processing and done. Each one tells you what to do next.
4. **Set the next step.** Decide who will fix it, and by when. Tell the tenant.
5. **Follow up.** Add a note after each call, visit or repair. If it takes several days, tell the tenant why.
6. **Close it.** When it is fixed, mark it done and tell the tenant.

## Common-area complaints

Some complaints are not about one tenant, such as the water tank, the staircase light or the gate. Record these against the property so they do not get lost.

## Keep a history

A list of past complaints shows which rooms or items cause repeat trouble. If the same geyser fails three times, replacing it is cheaper than repairing it again.

## Be polite and quick

Tenants forgive small problems when they feel heard. A quick reply and a clear date matter more than a perfect fix.
